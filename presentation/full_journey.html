01 — Phase 1: The Build

Replacing MLP with Conv1d UNet

What was the old model?

  • MLP-based UNet blocks — collapses temporal dimension
  • GoalCondDiffusionPolicy with simple Linear layers
  • 8.8M parameters, trained to loss = 0.034
  • Problem: MLP can't learn local temporal patterns in action chunks

What we replaced it with

  • Conv1dBlock: Conv1d → GroupNorm → Mish activation
  • Conv1dResBlock: two Conv1dBlocks + FiLM conditioning
  • Channels-first (B, C, T) processing throughout
  • FiLM: cond = time_mlp(t) + obs_embed + goal_embed, injected at every residual block
  • 11.7M parameters — same architecture in train + eval scripts

04 — The Turning Point

The questions that changed everything

Question 1
"How can success be 100%?"

Answer: cube_jitter=0.0 means blocks are always at the same position. The policy has memorized the path. 100% success is guaranteed for both — it tells us nothing.

Question 2
"What is my ultimate goal?"

Answer: The goal was never just "pick blocks successfully." Success is trivial. The goal is legibility: making the robot's intent obvious to a human observer early in the trajectory.

Question 3
"Did I achieve my goal?"

Honest answer: Partially. L_early improved 0.919→0.936. But L_early is a borrowed formula. The numbers are both high. And we had no independent human-interpretable verification.

Realization

We had the wrong metric conversation. The question was never "how do we train better" — it was "how do we prove legibility is improved, and what even is L_early?"

05 — The Key Question

"What is L_early intent? Explain with example"

This is the question that prompted all the subsequent investigation. Here's the answer:

L_early: formal definition

def l_early_intent(ee_traj, goals, true_goal_idx, early_frac=0.30): T = len(ee_traj) # total timesteps in episode end = int(T * 0.30) # look at first 30% only early = ee_traj[:end] # (end, 3) xyz positions # Gaussian likelihood: how close is EE to each goal? sigma = d_between_goals / (2√(2·ln2)) sq_dist = ||early - goal_k||² for each goal k p(goal_k | pos_t) = exp(-sq_dist / 2σ²) / Σ return mean( p(true_goal | pos_t) for t in [0..end] )

Source: evaluation/eval_legibility_diffuser.py lines 413–437, Dragan et al. HRI 2013

In plain English

  1. Cut the trajectory at 30%. If the episode is 300 steps, only look at steps 0–90.
  2. At each step: compute how close the end-effector is to goal A vs goal B (Gaussian distance).
  3. Convert to probability: closer to true goal = higher P(correct), using softmax over Gaussian distances.
  4. Average probability across all early steps = L_early.

The statement you saw:

"The score ∈[0,1] is absolute: 0.95 means '95% observer confidence in first 30% of motion' regardless of what other trajectories look like. You don't need a separate picking metric."

06 — L_early: Concrete Example

Two trajectories, same goal (pick left block)

Trajectory A — Straight/predictable L_early = 0.83

Robot goes straight down, then toward the left block

Left block [L]                 Right block [R]
           ↓ (start)
           ↓ ← equidistant from both
           ↓ ← still in middle
         ↙   ← 30% mark, now clearly left
      ↙
   [L] ← arrives

For first 30%: positions near the center means P(left) ≈ 0.5 for many steps → average ≈ 0.83

Trajectory B — Arced/legible L_early = 0.95

Robot arcs early toward the left block, then descends

Left block [L]                 Right block [R]
          ↓ (start)
       ↙ ← already leaning left!
     ↙ ← clearly closer to [L]
    ↓   ← 30% mark, very close to [L]
    ↓
   [L] ← arrives

For first 30%: positions consistently closer to left block → P(left) ≈ 0.9+ for most steps → average ≈ 0.95

Both trajectories reach the same goal, both succeed. But Trajectory B is legible — an observer can tell where it's going much earlier. The arc "wastes" some efficiency to broadcast intent.

08 — The "Absolute" Claim

Why L_early = 0.95 means something on its own

The statement explained

"The score ∈[0,1] is absolute: 0.95 means '95% observer confidence in first 30% of motion' regardless of what other trajectories look like. You don't need a separate picking metric."

What this means:

  • L_early is a Bayesian posterior probability, not a rank or percentile
  • It doesn't compare to other policies — it measures this trajectory's clarity
  • 0.95 = at the 30% mark, a Bayesian observer would be 95% sure of the correct goal
  • 0.50 = random chance (no information). 1.0 = perfect disambiguation from step 1.

Why "no separate picking metric"?

Since L_early is a probability of correctly identifying the goal, a high score simultaneously means:

  • The robot moved toward the right goal (picks the right block)
  • It did so early (visible before 30% of the trajectory)
  • The motion is unambiguous (high probability, not 0.55)

If L_early = 0.95, the robot both succeeds and is legible. One number encodes both.

The catch

  • L_early uses world-frame EE positions + goal coordinates
  • A human observer doesn't have this — they have camera pixels
  • L_early is a geometric proxy for human perception, not a measurement of it
  • This gap is what eventually led to the VLO proposal

10 — The Saturation Problem

Why full-video VLM scoring gives 0.9 for everything

What the VLM outputs look like

// baseline_ep000.mp4 "v2_legibility_score": 0.9, "v2_early_commitment": 0.2, "v2_path_shape": "direct", "v2_reasoning": "gripper moves directly towards block A from the start" // steered_left_ep000.mp4 (SAME policy steering to different goal) "v2_legibility_score": 0.9, "v2_early_commitment": 0.2, "v2_path_shape": "direct", "v2_reasoning": "gripper moves directly towards block B from the start"

Identical scores. Different policies. Different goals. No discrimination at all.

Root cause analysis

  • VLM sees the entire trajectory → knows the endpoint
  • Rationalizes backward: "it went there, so the intent was obvious"
  • This is hindsight availability bias — the same problem humans have when they know the outcome
  • Legibility is inherently prospective: "what would you infer at t=2 without knowing the rest?"
  • Any metric that sees the full video measures identification, not legibility

The fix: information restriction

Show the VLM only frames [0..t]. It literally cannot cheat because it doesn't have frames t+1, t+2, … This is the same design intuition behind Dragan's early_frac=0.30 — only evaluate the prefix.

Step 2

Step 3
Evaluated 20 episodes. All metrics looked good: L_early 0.9187→0.9355, success 100%.

13 — Where We Stand

What exists, what's proven, what's missing

Solid ground

  • Conv1d architecture: built, trained, 20× better loss
  • L_early: 0.919→0.936 (+1.8%), baseline→LegDiff
  • Full-video VLM = useless: proven across 3 experiments
  • Prefix VLM = works: proven across 2 experiments
  • L_early is absolute: 0.95 means 95% posterior confidence in first 30% — no comparison needed
  • Goal identification accuracy: v2 annotated-frame approach gets 97.5%

Open gaps

  • VLO on live policies: not yet run (only 2 demo arcs tested)
  • VLO: baseline vs LegDiff: no comparison exists
  • Human validation: no human legibility judgments collected
  • Statistical power: n=20 episodes, effect size 0.017 ± 0.03
  • Generalization: cube_jitter=0 only — fixed positions
  • CFG sweep: only cfg_scale=3.0 tested

The paper story

L_early (borrowed, geometric, proven to improve) + VLO (proposed, visual, prefix-based, proven to discriminate in pilot) = complementary metrics. LegDiff improves both. Full-video VLM failure is itself a contribution worth reporting.
