01 — The Problem

What is legibility and why does it matter?

The core idea (Dragan et al. 2013)

"A legible motion is one where an observer can quickly and confidently infer the robot's intended goal from early trajectory cues — before the robot is close enough that proximity alone gives it away." Dragan, A., Lee, K., Srinivasa, S. — Legibility and Predictability of Robot Motion. HRI 2013

Why it matters for TwoBlockPick

  • Two blocks. One gripper. Which block is it going for?
  • A predictable robot goes straight to goal — efficient but ambiguous mid-flight
  • A legible robot exaggerates its early arc toward the goal — slower, but intent is obvious
  • Humans co-working with the robot benefit from the earlier signal

Two-block setup — what distinguishes arcs

Arc index 0–4 = gentle curve (barely deviates). Arc 15–19 = maximal arc (exaggerated initial sweep toward goal). Our demo videos span this full range.

03 — Negative Result

Why full-video VLM scoring failed completely

What we observed

  • 15 episodes (baseline + steered-left + steered-right)
  • Every single video: legibility_score = 0.9
  • Every single video: early_commitment = 0.2
  • Every single video: path_shape = "direct"
  • VLM reasoning: "gripper moves directly toward block from start"

Root cause

  • Full trajectory reveals the endpoint — trivial to declare "legible in hindsight"
  • No temporal pressure. VLM doesn't distinguish early vs late commitment
  • Known in VLM evaluation literature as the ordering bias / hindsight availability problem
  • Fixed by information restriction: show only the prefix up to time t

Key lesson

Legibility is inherently a prospective measure — it asks what a naïve observer would have inferred at each moment. Any metric that sees the full trajectory is measuring identification-in-hindsight, not legibility. This is also why Dragan's L_early formula explicitly truncates the trajectory at a fixed fraction.

04 — Our Metric

VLO — VLM Legibility Onset

Formal definition

VLO(episode) = min { t ∈ {0,1,2,...,T} }
such that:
  VLM( frames[0..t] ) returns
  correct_side = True
  confidence ≥ 0.80

lower VLO = more legible

Protocol

  • Side-view camera (yaw=135°, known calibration)
  • Extract frames at t=0,1,2,…,T seconds
  • For each prefix clip [0..t]: send frames + short prompt to Gemini
  • VLM returns: pA, pB, cue, legible
  • VLO = first t where correct side ≥80%
  • Aggregate over N episodes per policy

Why this is better than L_early

  • No world-frame coordinates needed by evaluator
  • VLM provides a natural-language cue explaining why it decided
  • Threshold (80%) maps to "a typical human would be confident"

06 — Research Backing

What the literature actually supports

Dragan et al. HRI 2013 direct support

Legibility is defined as the probability that an observer correctly infers goal G from partial trajectory ξ[0,t]. Dragan proves that legible motions are qualitatively different from predictable ones — they deliberately deviate early. Dragan, A., Lee, K., Srinivasa, S. — Legibility and Predictability of Robot Motion

✓ Validates the partial-trajectory framing we adopted.
✓ Proves legibility requires explicit early deviation (our arc design).
✗ Uses analytic formula over geometric paths — we generalise to VLM.

Fu et al. IROS 2024 partial support

Video-based VLMs can assess robot trajectory quality when given structured prompts and temporal frame sequences. Goal-inference from visual cues is achievable without kinematic state access. Fu, Z. et al. — Mobile ALOHA: Learning Bimanual Mobile Manipulation with Low-Cost Whole-Body Teleoperation. 2024

✓ Supports that VLMs can interpret robot video with appropriate prompting.
✗ Does not validate VLM output against human legibility judgments specifically.

Ziebart et al. 2008 + Dragan 2013 — the L_early basis direct support

Maximum entropy IRL (Ziebart) gives the probabilistic framework; Dragan derives the legibility-optimal trajectory as one maximising P(G|ξ[0,t]) at every prefix. Our L_early is a direct approximation of this. Weakness: requires world-frame positions.

What is NOT backed honest gap

  • No paper validates Gemini-2.5-Flash legibility judgments against human subjects in manipulation tasks
  • VLO threshold (80%) is our choice — not grounded in a human study
  • n=2 arcs is a pilot, not a statistically significant result

07 — Honest Claims

What we can and cannot claim

Claims backed by our results

  • L_early improved: LegDiff Conv1d 0.936 vs baseline 0.919 over 20 episodes (both 100% success)
  • LegDiff reaches task in fewer steps: 307 vs 344 mean steps
  • VLO correctly discriminates high-legibility (arc19, VLO=0s) from low-legibility (arc01, VLO=1s) demo arcs
  • Full-video VLM scoring is useless — negative result is itself a contribution
  • Prefix-frame VLM protocol produces consistent, correct goal predictions across all time steps once the arc is visible
  • Conv1d LegDiff trains to 20× lower loss than MLP baseline (0.00175 vs 0.034)

Claims that need more work

  • "VLO correlates with human perception" — not validated. Needs human annotation study.
  • "VLO requires no geometric assumptions" — imprecise. Correct version: VLO requires no researcher-coded geometric formula. The VLM itself carries spatial priors.
  • VLO comparison baseline vs LegDiff — not yet run. Only 2 demo arcs tested, no live policy comparison exists.
  • Statistical significance — n=20 episodes is underpowered for L_early comparison (effect size ~0.017 ± 0.03)

09 — Next Steps

What needs to happen to make the claims solid

Short term (1–2 days)

  • Run VLO eval on 10 live episodes each: baseline + LegDiff Conv1d (≈50 API calls)
  • Report mean VLO ± std for both policies
  • Rerun L_early with n=50 episodes for statistical power
  • Add cube_jitter=0.01 to test generalisation

Medium term (to strengthen VLO claim)

  • CFG sweep w∈{0,1,2,3,5}: plot VLO vs success rate Pareto frontier
  • Run same VLO protocol on steered policy (already have 50-ep videos)
  • Consider minimal human annotation: 5 people, 10 clips, "when did you know?"
  • Compare VLO rank order vs L_early rank order across arcs — correlation = proxy validation

The honest paper story

We propose LegDiff (CFG-steered diffusion policy) + VLO (prefix-based VLM legibility onset) as a novel evaluation framework. LegDiff demonstrably improves L_early. VLO is a proposed human-interpretable complement to L_early that avoids geometric assumptions — validated as internally consistent in our pilot, but not yet compared to human judgments. That is a complete and defensible research contribution.

Summary

One-page takeaway

What we built

  • Conv1d LegDiff: goal-conditioned CFG diffusion, 20× lower loss than MLP
  • L_early evaluation: baseline 0.919 → LegDiff 0.936
  • VLO: prefix-based VLM legibility onset metric
  • Pilot proves VLO discriminates arc legibility classes
  • Identified and reported full-video VLM saturation failure

Key design decisions

  • Prefix frames, not full video — the only way to measure early intent
  • Side-view camera, not front — front view is noisy and unreliable for lateral goal classification
  • Annotated first frame — block markers give VLM spatial grounding without coordinate system assumptions
  • Threshold 80% — maps to "confident human inference"; below 80% is ambiguous

Honest gaps

  • No human legibility study (VLO = proposed proxy)
  • VLO vs LegDiff comparison not yet run
  • n=2 pilot arcs only — not statistically significant
  • Reward shaping (L_early training signal) is borrowed from Dragan
